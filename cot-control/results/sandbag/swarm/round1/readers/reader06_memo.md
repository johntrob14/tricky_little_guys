## Proposed coding distinctions

Keep explicit incorrect-answer intent, explicit rejection of that instruction, guessing for format/style reasons, no settled option, semantically implied option, earlier reasoned answer, terminal selected letter, and letter/content conflict separate. Add independent descriptions of the first visible error, repair or abandonment, changes in the selection criterion, and reference quality. These are descriptive dimensions, not a proposed detector.

## Interpretation and hypotheses to test

The solved-then-overridden case `base/safe/aware/602` shows why terminal CoT/final agreement alone can miss an explicit deliberate change. The instruction-rejecting but keyed-wrong `base/default/sandbag/2680` shows why an incorrect answer alone is not evidence of compliance with the sandbag instruction.

Generic noncommittal reasoning and generic but decisive reasoning should be distinguished in later comparisons. Instruction-induced guessing can occur without an expressed goal to be wrong. Criterion changes after identifying a relevant cue may merit paired comparisons, but the 4/4 calibration profiles already show natural counterparts to guessing, contradictions, incomplete calculations, and criterion changes. The present annotations do not infer hidden intent from those appearances.
