Steve CoT versus final output: offline pilot, completed2026-10-30 UTC.

Question: Can we distinguish the channels even though both read like finished explanations?
Answer: Yes in this ordinary-advice dataset. Opening phrasing and overall structure remain highly diagnostic. Local prose is substantially less separable than base or2e.

Data: results/iei/{base,mt1b-s150,mt2e-s170}/baseline.jsonl under /home/john/code/tlg-cascade/cot-control. All175 rollouts/model,35 scenarios with5 repetitions each. Same questions and rep IDs across models; all stop normally. Both channels nonempty. Bare ordinary advice prompts, no control or nudge. Exact source hashes in results.json. No new subject-model sampling or external judge calls; Codex usage cost unmeasured.

Design: logistic regression on lowercased word-unigram TF-IDF; vocabulary fit inside each training fold, minimum document frequency2, sublinear term frequency, L2-normalized vectors, fixed C=1. Punctuation and Markdown delimiters excluded by tokenizer. Five folds hold out entire scenarios: all5 repetitions of a scenario stay together. Same folds for each model. Seed20261030; no hyperparameter tuning. Separate classifiers per model and text view. Paired scoring assigns final to the higher-scoring of the two texts from one rollout; chance50%. Also report independent single-text channel classification. No human or LLM semantic channel judges were used.

Views: full text; length only; matched opening excerpts; matched excerpts at a random relative position. Excerpts have min(60,shortest of all6 matched channel texts) whitespacewords, range34-60; same length and relative position across model/channel within a question/rep. Thus excerpt comparisons eliminate length differences. Full-text unigram features can still indirectly encode text length and formatting conventions. Random excerpt sampling happens before fitting. Paired95% intervals bootstrap35 scenarios10000times; this conditions on fitted out-of-fold predictions and does not include refitting/split uncertainty.

Interpretation: sampled Steve CoTs begin by introducing factors and comparing options; finals often lead with a verdict and reorganize or condense the explanation. Both can directly address the requester. Descriptive full-data feature weights favor 'potential','however','could','factors','considerations' in CoT; 'why','tl','dr','pick','bottom','quick' in final. These feature weights illustrate correlation and were not used to choose classifier settings. This supports 'two distinguishable answer-like forms' and is compatible with earlier99.3% finished-prose rating. It does not show that CoT and final have identical information, that the final is a faithful summary, or that CoT has no computational role.
