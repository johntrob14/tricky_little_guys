The reasoning channel ignores what the answer channel follows

Asked to write its reasoning in a language, a model almost never does; asked to explain its answer in that language, it almost always does. The same holds when the style is already on the page: prefilling 80 words of the model's own reasoning, rewritten into the required format, does not make the continuation keep it.

Training on the language of the reasoning creates general control

GRPO that rewards only writing the reasoning in a requested language (times correctness, with a length penalty; no CoT-Control types, no judges) lifts every rule-graded type. The same reward on the language of the final answer does nothing. The gain shrinks under an unfamiliar instruction wording, and a run without the reference template gains less.

CoT-control training makes the reasoning follow the style on the page

With 80 words of base reasoning restyled into the required format, CoT-control-trained checkpoints continue in style; base, length, effort and output-control checkpoints do not, and neither does the language-trained run, which follows instructions without this persistence.

What goes with control

Across Neel's 34 open models, CoT control barely tracks NCRI; the one label that does is how often the model mentions the instruction. Models that move the constraint into the answer attempt it in the reasoning less.

Limits

  • One training seed per run; lang2 and the answer-language run were stopped early for budget. The answer-language run is not prompt-matched to lang1 and starts near ceiling.
  • The held-out wording changes the frame, the term, the core wording and the synonym lists at once, so how much of the transfer comes from the shared template is not identified.
  • All causal results are on gpt-oss-120b; the cross-model language check is correlational and stores only the first 300 characters of each trace.
  • Training changed accuracy too: CoT accuracy falls with control, and one control-trained checkpoint lost unconstrained no-CoT accuracy.
