# Power analysis by sol-ncri/power

Question: how much does 34 models x 108 binary items at roughly 3% success constrain a relation with NCRI?

## Results

- Independent raw-file enumeration (`verify.py`): 34 graded files, 3,631 rows, 96 successes, 13 models with zero successes. 33 files have 108 rows; one has 67. This is NOT yet the appendix's selected witness set: lead agent reports analyze.py uses 54 nested-witness items for GLM-4.5, yielding 96/3,618. My simulations use the nominal 108 trials per model and calibrate mean rate to all available graded rows, 96/3,631 = 2.64390%. This tiny denominator difference is immaterial for the power question; model selection must follow the lead agent's audit. See `baseline.csv`.
- With 34 independent model observations, a conventional two-sided Spearman test at alpha .05 needs |rho| > .33879 using its usual t approximation. In 15,000 replicate Gaussian-copula simulations, a mean observed population-like Spearman .43098 gives 75.27% power and .48010 gives 85.70% power. Thus roughly .45-.48 observed correlation is needed for 80% power. A mean observed .28538 has only 38.63% power. The absence of significance has weak force against modest relationships.
- Count noise changes latent-to-observed correlations. In a logistic-normal model matched to observed between-model rate variance (sigma 1.06036), latent Spearman .28349 becomes observed .22668 and power 25.43%; latent .47989 becomes observed .38132 and power 62.89%; latent .57751 becomes observed .46163 and power 81.80%. In a narrower true-rate distribution (sigma .5), latent .58026 becomes observed .35532 with 56.34% power; latent .78551 is needed for 84.56% power. These are explicit model assumptions, not estimates that a hidden relationship exists.
- A homogeneous 2.64390% binomial success probability predicts 1.882 zero models among 34, versus 13 observed. The moment-based logistic-normal fit predicts about 8.54 zero models; sigma 1.5 predicts about 12.90 but has more rate variance. The empirical distribution is heterogeneous and the exact lower tail matters. Overall variance-based reliability .82155 does not certify good rank resolution at zero.
- A model with 0/108 successes still has an exact one-sided 95% binomial upper bound 2.73571%. Zero does not distinguish true zero from a low rate, even under independent Bernoulli assumptions.
- In an illustrative hurdle sensitivity (25% structural-zero models, independent of NCRI; the remaining active models logistic-normal sigma 1 and their mean increased to maintain overall 2.64390%), active-model Gaussian-copula correlation .6 yields overall latent Spearman .32905, observed .31286, power 45.17%, and about 12.86 observed zero models. Active correlation .9 yields overall latent .50678 and 81.63% power. Thus a strong relationship among active models can be masked by an independent zero-generating mechanism. These simulations do not demonstrate that Qwen has such a mechanism.

## Exact simulation and limitations

`power.py`, seed 2601026, 15,000 replications per grid cell, fixed normal-quantile NCRI-like predictor for 34 models. Generate a latent Gaussian correlated with that predictor, transform through expit(intercept + sigma * z); choose intercept by deterministic quantile integration to match pooled probability; draw independent Binomial(108,p) counts per model. Compute average-rank Spearman and reject when its absolute value exceeds the usual t critical value. The null rejection rate is around .05 in all three sigma scenarios. Parameters preserve approximately, not exactly, the mean in fixed-predictor non-null simulations. Monte Carlo uncertainty in estimated power is at most about .0041 standard error. `hurdle.py` adds independently drawn structural zeros.

These are conditional-design sensitivity analyses. Trials are actually a fixed Latin-square bank at temperature zero, not literal IID repeated coin flips: item difficulty, shared prompts, grader errors, and type-specific effects may increase or decrease noise. NCRI itself is treated as error-free. Both item correlation and NCRI measurement error may reduce power further. The 34 models also share training families, violating independent model sampling. A common-family effect can create high apparent power while supplying little evidence for a general model-level relation. Collapsing to labs reduces power: see `cluster_sensitivity.csv` for explicit 12-independent-unit illustrative simulations; this is a sensitivity case, not a count of the audited dataset's labs. The design-effect formula in summary.json is illustrative only and should not be treated as estimated effective sample size.
